01 The problem

Same doctor. Same checks. Three parties who never compare notes.

A hospital verifies the license. A payer verifies the license. The board issued the license. Everyone runs the same primary source checks in their own silo, on their own clock, and the provider waits on all of them.

  1. 60–180daysInitial credentialing, start to finish
  2. 120 / 90daysNCQA primary source verification time windows
  3. ~120daysHow often providers re-attest on CAQH
  4. NPI ≠ clearedHaving a number doesn't mean you're credentialed (CMS)

Cited industry context, not a RushPoppy ROI claim.

02 Why not symplr?

Your credentialing team isn't slow. Your architecture is.

symplr built a better vault. We think the vault is the bottleneck.

Centralized platforms pull every file into one record and verify from that copy. Every party feeds the same pipe, and every copy is one more thing to guard. RushPoppy runs the other way. The orchestrator asks every party at once, over Flower SuperGrid. Each one answers from its own records. Only the answer moves.

The vault model compared with the grid model

Asking two parties a question should take seconds. On Flower, it does.

~20sto collect claims from two SuperNodes when sources agree
~30swhen they disagree and the orchestrator re-asks the owner
~6sto apply a human decision once it's made

Measured once on one laptop: local TLS SuperLink, two authenticated SuperNodes, synthetic data, nodes already online. Not a SuperGrid benchmark and not an end-to-end credentialing time. symplr is named as a category example; see their solutions for payer leaders. RushPoppy is not that suite and does not replace it today.

03 The trick

Files stay home. Only the answer travels.

Each SuperNode keeps its own records: DEA numbers, work history, banking, peer review. What leaves is a typed claim with a value, a confidence, and a pointer to the evidence. Nobody builds a copy of anyone else's file.

04 The handoff

Kickoff to receipt in five steps. One of them is a person.

  1. 01 · kickoff

    Ask

    PoppyOrchestrator gets one request: verify SYNTH-NPI-1999999999 for network X.

  2. 02 · claim_request

    Fan out

    One push over Flower SuperGrid reaches HospitalCred and PayerEnrollment together. Each is asked only for the claims it owns.

  3. 03 · claims_aggregated

    Collect

    Six typed claims come back, each with a value, a confidence, and a source reference. No PDFs.

  4. 04 · hitl_pause

    Decide human

    A person approves, escalates, or rejects in Flower Chat. Nothing approves itself.

  5. 05 · claim_receipt

    Prove it

    Only after an approve: an auditable receipt with every claim and every stage of the run.

05 The hard case

When sources disagree, we don't average. We ask again.

No majority vote. No silent override. RushPoppy goes back to the party that owns the claim, once, with the exact period in question. Then a person decides with both answers in front of them.

  1. A node never answers for a claim it doesn't own.PayerEnrollment can't return work history. It raises a dispute against it instead.
  2. The owner gets asked once. There is no third round.The second answer decides the status: agreed, explained, or unresolved.

  4. Unresolved means no approve.Escalate or reject. The run retains the dispute; neither action issues a receipt.
